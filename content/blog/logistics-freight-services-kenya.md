# Logistics & Freight Services in Kenya: Complete Guide 2026

## Introduction

Logistics is the invisible engine of the Kenyan economy. Every product on a shelf, every piece of imported machinery, every parcel delivered to a doorstep, and every business relocation depends on the movement and management of goods. As Kenya solidifies its position as East Africa's commercial hub, reliable logistics and freight services have never been more critical.

This comprehensive guide explores logistics and freight services in Kenya—from countrywide haulage and last-mile delivery to office and home moving, clearing and forwarding, and warehousing. Whether you are a business shipping goods, an individual moving home, or an importer navigating customs, this guide provides practical, locally relevant knowledge to help you move goods efficiently, safely, and cost-effectively.

## Understanding the Kenyan Logistics Landscape

### Kenya as a Logistics Hub

- **Port of Mombasa**: The gateway for East and Central Africa—Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, DRC, South Sudan
- **Standard Gauge Railway (SGR)**: Mombasa-Nairobi freight and passenger rail; expanding logistics capacity
- **Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA)**: Regional air cargo hub
- **LAPSSET**: Long-term vision linking Lamu to Ethiopia and South Sudan
- **Road network**: Nairobi-Nakuru-Eldoret corridor, Mombasa-Nairobi highway (ongoing expressway developments)

### Key Logistics Routes in Kenya

| Route | Importance | Distance |
|-------|------------|----------|
| Mombasa – Nairobi | Main import/export corridor | ~500 km |
| Nairobi – Nakuru | Regional distribution | ~160 km |
| Nairobi – Kisumu | Western Kenya / East Africa transit | ~350 km |
| Nairobi – Eldoret | North Rift / Uganda | ~310 km |
| Nairobi – Mombasa | Coastal corridor | ~500 km |
| Nairobi – Isiolo/Marsabit | Northern Kenya | 300-600 km |

### Types of Logistics Providers

**Freight Forwarders**: Manage international and domestic freight movement (ships, air, road, rail), documentation, customs clearance.

**Couriers**: Time-sensitive small parcels (documents, e-commerce orders).

**Trucking/3PL (Third-Party Logistics)**: Full-service transportation, warehousing, distribution.

**Last-Mile Providers**: Final delivery to homes/businesses (e-commerce focus).

**Specialized Movers**: Office/home relocation with packing and handling.

## Countrywide Haulage

### What is Haulage?

The transport of goods by truck between locations—the backbone of domestic freight. Haulage moves everything from containerized imports to bulk commodities and consumer goods.

### Types of Trucks and Their Uses

**Light Trucks (1-7 tons)**:
- Pickup trucks, small box trucks, vans
- Local distribution, small consignments
- Retail restocking, last-mile

**Medium Trucks (7-15 tons)**:
- Box bodies, curtain-siders
- Regional distribution
- Palletized goods

**Heavy Trucks (15-40 tons)**:
- Flatbeds, lowbed trailers, tankers, tippers
- Bulk commodities, machinery, construction materials
- Container transport

**Specialized Equipment**:
- **Lowbeds**: Heavy machinery, equipment
- **Refrigerated trucks**: Perishables, pharmaceuticals
- **Tankers**: Fuel, liquids, milk
- **Tipper trucks**: Bulk aggregates, sand
- **Flatbeds**: Over-sized loads

### How Haulage Is Priced

**Pricing models**:
- **Per kilometre**: Common for distance-based moves
- **Per tonne**: Based on cargo weight
- **Per trip/route**: Fixed route pricing (e.g., Nairobi-Mombasa)
- **Per day**: For equipment/personnel hire

**Cost factors**:
- Distance
- Weight/volume
- Truck type required
- Load/unload complexity
- Fuel prices (directly affect rates)
- Return journey (empty return leg is often priced in)
- Insurance

### Working with a Haulage Provider

**What to confirm**:
- Truck availability and condition
- Driver qualifications and experience
- Insurance coverage (cargo insurance, third-party liability)
- Tracking capability (GPS)
- Expected transit time
- Loading/unloading arrangements
- Payment terms

**Documentation**: Waybills (delivery notes), receipts, insurance certificates, driver contact.

## Last-Mile Delivery

### The Challenge of Last-Mile

The "last mile"—from a distribution center to the final customer—is often the most expensive and complex part of delivery. It involves:
- Traffic congestion (especially Nairobi)
- Address finding and customer availability
- Failed delivery attempts (rerouting cost)
- Security considerations
- Temperature/time-sensitive goods

### Last-Mile Options

**In-house fleets**: Full control, higher fixed cost—for high-volume businesses.

**Third-party couriers**: Variable cost, scale benefits—for growing businesses.

**Rider/boda networks**: Fast urban delivery (food, small parcels).

**Automated/tech platforms**: App-based dispatch and tracking.

**Click-and-collect / pickup points**: Reduce delivery failure (customer collects).

### Last-Mile Best Practices for Businesses

- **Accurate address data**: Invest in address verification
- **Time windows**: Offer and respect delivery windows
- **Tracking**: Provide real-time tracking to customers
- **Delivery confirmation**: Proof of delivery (photo/signature)
- **Returns handling**: Clear reverse logistics process
- **Peak planning**: Capacity planning for high seasons (festive, back-to-school)

### Last-Mile Costs (indicative, Nairobi 2026)

- Courier within Nairobi: KES 200 - 800 per drop (boda/vehicle)
- Same-day city delivery: KES 500 - 1,500
- Out-of-town delivery: KES 1,000 - 4,000
- Bulk/last-mile freight: negotiated per load

## Office and Home Moving

**Why professional office moving matters**:
- Business continuity (minimize downtime)
- IT and equipment handling (disassembly, safe transport, setup)
- Furniture disassembly/reassembly
- Sensitive documentation and data security
- Insurance protection during transit

**The office relocation process**:
1. **Survey and plan**: Inventory, layout, timeline
2. **Budget and proposal**: Detailed quote
3. **Pre-move preparation**: Packing plan, labeling, IT plan, downtime schedule
4. **Moving day**: Professional crew, supervised loading/unloading, transport
5. **Setup**: Furniture reassembly, IT reconnection, layout placement
6. **Post-move**: Snagging, unpacking assistance, cleanup

**Office move cost (indicative)**:
- Small office (10 staff): KES 30,000 - 80,000
- Mid-size (30 staff): KES 80,000 - 200,000
- Large (100+ staff): KES 200,000 - 600,000+
- Factors: distance, quantity, disassembly needs, packing scope

**Minimizing disruption**:
- Move over a weekend or holiday
- Detailed labeling (room, location)
- IT provider coordinated for network cutover
- Clear communication with staff

### Home Moving

**Process**:
1. Quote based on inventory and distance
2. Packing (DIY or professional packing service)
3. Loading
4. Transport
5. Unloading and placement
6. Unpacking (optional)

**Costs (indicative)**:
- Local move (1-2 bedrooms): KES 15,000 - 40,000
- Local move (3-4 bedrooms): KES 40,000 - 80,000
- Intercity (3-4 bedrooms): KES 80,000 - 200,000
- Packing materials/boxes: KES 5,000 - 20,000
- Professional packing service: extra

**Tips for a smooth move**:
- Declutter before moving (save space and cost)
- Label boxes by room and priority
- Pack a "first night" box (essentials)
- Confirm access at both properties (parking, stairs, lifts)
- Insure valuable items
- Book well in advance (weekends/months end are busy)

### What to Look For in a Mover

- Registered company with proper insurance
- References and portfolio
- Detailed written quote (no vague estimates)
- Trained, professional crews
- Clear terms (payment, liability, damage claims)
- Equipment quality (trucks, packing materials)

## Clearing and Forwarding

### What is Clearing and Forwarding?

**Clearing**: Handling customs clearance of imports/exports—documentation, duties, and release of goods.

**Forwarding**: Managing the international movement of cargo (sea, air, land) and coordinating transport.

### Why You Need a Clearing and Forwarding Agent

Importing involves complex procedures: documentation, tariffs, valuation, inspections, and regulatory compliance. A professional agent:
- Navigates customs procedures correctly (avoids delays and penalties)
- Ensures correct duty/tax classification (saves money)
- Coordinates all documentation (import declaration forms, permits, certificates)
- Handles inspections and release
- Arranges inland transport (port to warehouse/plant)
- Advises on regulatory requirements (standards, permits for specific goods)

### Key Documents in Importing

- **Proforma invoice**: From supplier
- **Commercial invoice**: Final transaction record
- **Bill of Lading (B/L) / Airway Bill (AWB)**: Transport contract
- **Packing list**: Contents details
- **Import Declaration Form (IDF)**: KRA requirement
- **Certificate of Origin**: For duty preferences
- **Standards compliance**: KEBS verification/import standards marks where applicable
- **Phytosanitary/veterinary certificates**: For agricultural/animal products
- **Excisable goods permits**: Where applicable

### Customs Duty and Taxes

- **Import duty**: Varies by HS code and country of origin (EAC Common External Tariff)
- **VAT**: 16% standard rate on most imports
- **Excise duty**: On specific goods (e.g., alcohol, fuel, electronics)
- **IDF fee**: KRA import declaration fee
- **Other levies**: Port charges, storage, handling

**Note**: Rates and regulations change. Always verify current rates with your clearing agent or KRA.

### Common Importing Pitfalls

- **Incorrect classification**: Paying wrong duty (underpayment penalties, overpayment cost)
- **Missing documentation**: Delays, demurrage costs
- **Under-invoicing/over-invoicing**: Customs scrutiny and penalties
- **Permit omissions**: For regulated goods (health, standards, environment)
- **Storage/demurrage**: Containers held at port accrue charges
- **Fraud**: Unscrupulous agents misrepresenting charges

### Choosing a Clearing and Forwarding Agent

- Licensed and registered (Kenya International Freight and Warehousing Association - KIFWA)
- Experience with your goods type
- Transparent fee structure
- Track record of on-time clearance
- Communication and accountability
- Referenced clients

## Cargo Insurance

### Why Insure Cargo

Transport involves risk: accidents, theft, damage, and natural events. Cargo insurance protects the value of goods in transit. Without it, a single loss can be catastrophic for a business.

### Types of Cover

- **Institute Cargo Clauses (A)**: All-risk (broadest cover)
- **(B)**: More limited (specified events)
- **(C)**: Most limited (major events)
- **Total loss only**: Narrowest

### Key Considerations

- Insure cargo value (plus freight and duty for full recovery)
- Understand exclusions (inherent vice, delay, war, etc.)
- Perishables and fragile goods need specific covers
- Declare accurately (under-declaration = proportional recovery)
- Proof of loss procedures

**Indicative premiums**: 0.2% - 0.5% of cargo value typically (varies by route, cargo, mode).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to transport goods in Kenya?

**Indicative rates (2026)**:
- **Intra-city (Nairobi)**: KES 3,000 - 15,000 per trip (small to medium truck)
- **Nairobi - Mombasa**: KES 60,000 - 120,000 per truck (varies by size)
- **Nairobi - Kisumu**: KES 50,000 - 100,000
- **Nairobi - Eldoret**: KES 45,000 - 90,000
- **Per tonne**: KES 3,000 - 8,000 per tonne depending on route
- **Courier parcels**: KES 300 - 2,000

Rates fluctuate with fuel prices and demand. Always obtain written quotes.

### How long does delivery take within Kenya?

**Indicative**:
- **Nairobi same-day/next-day**: Same-day for priority, 1-2 days standard
- **Major towns (Nakuru, Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret)**: 1-3 days
- **Rural/countrywide**: 2-7 days depending on location and road access
- **International (via Mombasa)**: 1-4 weeks depending on origin and clearance

Confirm expected timelines with your provider; remote locations take longer.

### How do I choose between courier, trucking, and haulage?

**Match the service to the need**:
- **Documents/small parcels, urgent**: Courier
- **E-commerce orders (small packages)**: Last-mile/courier
- **Larger consignments (pallet+ )**: Trucking/haulage
- **Bulk/machinery**: Heavy haulage
- **International**: Freight forwarder + haulage

**Key questions**: Size/weight, distance, speed required, special handling, budget.

### Do I need insurance for my cargo?

**Strongly recommended—yes.** Transport risks (theft, accidents, damage) are real. Cargo insurance:
- Protects cargo value during transit
- Often required by lenders/parties
- Provides peace of mind
- Cost: typically 0.2-0.5% of cargo value

**Check**: Whether your provider includes basic cover or you need separate cargo insurance (many businesses under-insure—verify your policy's limits and exclusions).

### How do I move my office without losing business days?

**Plan a structured relocation**:
1. Start planning 4-8 weeks ahead
2. Professional survey and schedule
3. Move over a weekend/off-peak
4. Label everything by room/location
5. Coordinate IT cutover in advance
6. Communicate the plan to staff and clients
7. Have a contingency for any operational systems
8. Confirm new site readiness (power, internet, security)

**Pro-Tech tip**: Many businesses schedule a phased move—critical teams first, then the rest—to minimize downtime.

### What are the documents required for importing goods?

**Core documents**:
- Commercial invoice
- Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
- Packing list
- Import Declaration Form (IDF)
- Certificate of origin (for duty preferences)
- Permits/certificates (depending on goods: KEBS, KEPHIS, pharmacy boards, etc.)

**Note**: Requirements depend on the goods, origin, and value. A clearing and forwarding agent handles the full documentation process correctly.

### What is the difference between clearing and forwarding?

**Clearing** = customs clearance: preparing/submitting documentation, paying duties, obtaining release of goods from customs.

**Forwarding** = the broader coordination of international and domestic cargo movement: booking, transport, consolidation, documentation, insurance, and managing the journey end-to-end.

**In practice**, firms often combine both ("clearing and forwarding agents")—handling your import/export end-to-end.

### How do freight rates get calculated?

**Rates** depend on:
- **Distance** (route)
- **Weight and/or volume** (the greater "chargeable weight" usually applies)
- **Truck/container type** required
- **Loading/unloading** complexity
- **Fuel prices** (surcharges)
- **Mode** (road, rail, air, sea)
- **Timing/urgency**
- **Return journey** considerations

**Get itemized quotes** to compare providers fairly.

### Can I track my shipment?

**Yes, in most professional operations**:
- GPS vehicle tracking (real-time)
- Online portals/links for customers
- SMS/email notifications at milestones
- Proof-of-delivery confirmation

**Ask your provider** what tracking is available before engaging. This is a standard expectation for modern logistics.
