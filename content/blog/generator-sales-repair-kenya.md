# Generator Sales & Repair in Kenya: Complete Guide 2026

## Introduction

Reliable electricity is the backbone of modern business, and in Kenya, the realities of load-shedding, grid instability, and expanding demand mean that a dependable backup power solution is no longer a luxury—it is a business necessity. From retail shops and medical facilities to manufacturing plants, offices, and apartment blocks, generators provide the continuity that keeps operations running, data protected, and livelihoods secured.

This comprehensive guide explores the world of generator sales and repair in Kenya, covering everything from choosing the right generator for your needs to maintenance practices, repair services, and the installation of critical accessories such as automatic transfer switches (ATS). Whether you are purchasing your first generator, upgrading an aging unit, or seeking professional servicing, this guide provides practical, locally relevant insight.

## Understanding Your Power Requirements

### Why Kenyan Businesses Need Generators

**Grid Reliability**: Kenya Power has made significant investments in expanding and stabilizing the national grid, yet power interruptions remain a reality across the country. Scheduled maintenance, weather-related outages, and infrastructure failures all contribute to downtime.

**Business Continuity**: An hour of power loss can cost a retail business thousands in lost sales, a hospital untold risk, and a manufacturer hours of lost production.

**Data and Equipment Protection**: Sudden power loss damages sensitive electronics, servers, and machinery. A generator with an ATS provides seamless transition, protecting both hardware and data.

**Emergencies**: Backup power for medical facilities, water pumping, and security systems is critical.

### Estimating Your Power Needs

**Calculate Total Load**:
- List all equipment that must run during an outage
- Note the running (continuous) wattage for each
- Note the starting (surge) wattage—motors, compressors, and pumps draw 2-3x their running load at start-up
- Sum the continuous loads; ensure the generator handles the largest single surge plus the running load of everything else

**Example Calculation** (small office):
- 15 computers (150W each) = 2,250W
- 5 LED lights (10W each) = 50W
- Server + network equipment = 1,500W
- Security cameras + alarm = 300W
- Water pump (running 750W, surge 1,900W) = 1,900W surge
- Total continuous = 4,100W; largest surge 1,900W → generator rated 6-8 kVA is appropriate

**Overload Safety**: Do not size a generator to run at 100% capacity continuously. A safety margin of 20-30% is standard practice, ensuring the generator runs at 70-80% load—the sweet spot for fuel efficiency and longevity.

### Generator Types

**Portable Generators**:
- **Rating**: Typically 2-10 kVA
- **Fuel**: Petrol or diesel
- **Best for**: Homes, small shops, occasional use, construction sites
- **Pros**: Affordable, easy to move, simple operation
- **Cons**: Manual start, limited runtime, louder, less efficient at continuous load

**Standby Generators** (fixed installation):
- **Rating**: 5 kVA and above, up to industrial megawatt units
- **Fuel**: Diesel (most common for larger units), gas (LPG/natural gas)
- **Best for**: Commercial, industrial, medical, residential backup
- **Pros**: Automatic start via ATS, higher reliability, better fuel efficiency, quieter enclosures
- **Cons**: Higher initial cost, requires professional installation, dedicated fuel management

**Prime vs. Standby Rating**:
- **Prime power**: Can run at full rated output continuously (unlimited hours)
- **Standby power**: Designed for occasional use (typically 200-500 hours per year); running continuously shortens lifespan
- **Critical**: Choose a generator rated for your intended duty cycle. A hospital generator running daily needs prime-rated; a weekend home generator may be fine with standby-rated.

## Choosing the Right Generator

### Key Specifications to Compare

**Power Rating (kVA/kW)**:
- The single most important number—sized to your total load plus margin
- Note: kVA vs kW difference matters for power factor; for typical loads, kVA × 0.8 ≈ kW

**Engine Type**:
- **Diesel**: Most common for 10 kVA+; better fuel economy, longer life, greater durability
- **Petrol**: Smaller units; cheaper to buy, more expensive to run per kWh
- **Gas**: Cleaner emissions, cheaper fuel where piped gas available; installation more complex

**Engine Brand**:
- **Cummins**: Industry standard for larger commercial/industrial; extensive dealer network in Kenya
- **Perkins**: Common in African market; parts readily available
- **Caterpillar (CAT)**: Premium brand; high-end industrial/prime power
- **Kubota, Yanmar**: Reliable small diesel engines
- **Chinese brands (SDE, FG Wilson, etc.)**: Competitive pricing; check parts availability before purchase

**Voltage Configuration**:
- Single phase (230V) for most domestic/small commercial
- Three phase (415V) for industrial equipment and larger loads
- **Critical**: Verify your equipment requires single or three-phase before purchase

**Fuel Tank Capacity**:
- Determines runtime between refueling
- Larger tanks reduce refueling frequency; external day tanks (with transfer pumps) extend runtime for critical facilities

**Noise Enclosures**:
- **Open units**: No soundproofing; for external locations with no noise constraints
- **Canopied/enclosed**: Soundproofed canopy; essential for residential areas, hospitals, hotels, and noise-sensitive commercial zones
- **Noise levels**: Residential areas in Nairobi typically restrict noise; enclosed units rated at 70-75 dBA at 7m are standard for urban installations

## Generator Installation

### Site Requirements

**Ventilation**: Generators produce exhaust gases and heat. Adequate airflow is essential for engine cooling and preventing carbon monoxide accumulation.

**Foundation**: A level concrete pad (typically 100-150mm thick) sized for the unit plus service access. Vibration isolation mounts reduce noise and structural transfer.

**Clearances**: Maintain recommended clearances around the unit for access, airflow, and maintenance (typically 1-1.5m on service side).

**Fuel Storage**: 
- Diesel fuel storage regulations in Kenya require proper bunded tanks for larger installations
- Underground vs. above-ground tanks; day tank systems for critical facilities
- Fuel quality management: water and sediment accumulation in tanks damages injectors and pumps

**Exhaust Routing**: Properly supported exhaust system, directed away from doors, windows, and air intakes.

**Electrical Integration**:
- Properly rated cabling from generator to main distribution panel
- Changeover switch (manual or ATS) between grid and generator
- Earth grounding system—critical for safety
- Surge protection for sensitive loads

### Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS)

**What is an ATS?** A device that automatically switches the load between mains supply and generator.

**How it Works**:
1. Mains supply fails → ATS detects loss within seconds
2. Generator start signal sent → generator cranks and starts automatically
3. Generator reaches correct voltage/frequency → ATS transfers load
4. Mains restored → ATS waits for stabilization period (typically 30 seconds - 3 minutes)
5. Load transferred back to mains → generator runs a cool-down period then shuts down

**ATS Benefits**:
- No human intervention required during outages
- Faster restoration (typically 10-30 seconds total)
- Protects equipment from brownouts and power instability
- Ideal for 24/7 operations (hospitals, data centers, security systems)

**ATS Installation Cost in Kenya**: KES 45,000 - 250,000 depending on rating and features.

## Generator Maintenance

### Preventive Maintenance

Regular maintenance is the single most important factor in generator reliability. A well-maintained generator can last 20,000-30,000+ hours; a neglected one fails when you need it most.

**Daily / Weekly Checks**:
- Fuel level
- Engine oil level
- Coolant level
- Battery voltage and electrolyte
- Visual inspection for leaks (fuel, oil, coolant)
- Check air filter indicator

**Monthly Checks**:
- Run the generator under load for 30-60 minutes (especially important for standby units)
- Check and tighten belts
- Inspect hoses for cracks
- Test ATS function and battery charging system

**Scheduled Maintenance (every 250-500 engine hours or 6 months)**:
- Engine oil and filter change
- Fuel filter replacement
- Air filter cleaning/replacement
- Coolant level check and top-up
- Valve clearance check (per manufacturer schedule)
- Injector inspection
- Battery load test
- Torque check on major fasteners

**Annual / 1000+ hour maintenance**:
- Coolant flush and replacement
- Thermostat check
- Injector service
- Turbocharger inspection
- Governor calibration
- Load bank testing
- Full operational test under load

### Common Generator Problems and Diagnostics

**Generator Fails to Start**:
- **Battery**: Dead or low; check voltage, connections, corrosion
- **Fuel**: Empty tank, blocked fuel filter, air in fuel lines, stale fuel
- **Starting system**: Faulty starter motor, solenoid, or starting circuit
- **Safety shutdowns**: Low oil pressure or high coolant temperature cutouts may be tripped
- **Glow plugs/preheat**: Failed (diesel)

**Generator Starts But Won't Produce Power**:
- **Automatic voltage regulator (AVR)**: Faulty—common failure point
- **Excitation system**: Fault
- **Frequency not in range**: Engine speed governor issue
- **Output breaker**: Tripped
- **Wiring**: Loose or damaged connections

**Generator Runs Roughly / Loses Power**:
- **Fuel supply**: Blocked filter, air leak, water in fuel
- **Injectors**: Faulty or clogged
- **Air supply**: Blocked air filter
- **Compression**: Engine wear, valve issues
- **Governor**: Misadjusted or faulty

**Generator Overheats**:
- **Coolant**: Low level, leaks, blocked radiator
- **Radiator**: Dirt and debris blocking airflow
- **Fan/belts**: Faulty or loose
- **Pump**: Water pump failure

**Excessive Smoke**:
- **Black smoke**: Overloaded, blocked air filter, poor combustion
- **White smoke**: Cold start (normal when cold), coolant leak into cylinders, fuel injection issues
- **Blue smoke**: Burning oil—engine wear, worn rings/valve seals

### Diesel Engine Overhaul

**When is an overhaul needed?**
- Excessive oil consumption
- Loss of compression/power
- Excessive blow-by or unusual engine noise
- High wear indicated by oil analysis
- Major internal damage

**Overhaul scope** (top vs. full overhaul):
- **Top overhaul**: Cylinder head, valves, gaskets, injector service
- **Full overhaul**: Complete rebuild including pistons, rings, bearings, liners

**Overhaul cost in Kenya**: Typically KES 80,000 - 500,000+ depending on engine size and extent. Always get a detailed quote after diagnostic inspection.

**Cost consideration**: For older generators, compare overhaul cost against replacement cost. A unit approaching its economic life may justify replacement over overhaul.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I run my standby generator?

**Monthly minimum**: Run under load for 30-60 minutes. This keeps the engine lubricated, battery charged, and verifies system function. A generator that never runs will fail when called upon.

**Monthly run also**:
- Circulates fuel through the system
- Keeps seals and gaskets lubricated
- Verifies ATS operation
- Prevents moisture buildup in the fuel tank

### What is the lifespan of a generator?

**Well-maintained**: 20,000-30,000+ hours of operation (diesel units commonly exceed this).

**Factors affecting lifespan**:
- Regular preventive maintenance (most important factor)
- Load profile (running at consistent, moderate load is better than frequent cold starts)
- Fuel quality
- Operating environment (clean, ventilated, protected from elements)
- Engine brand and quality

**Standby generators** used rarely can last 15-25+ years with proper care, since engine hours remain low.

### How do I size a generator for my home or business?

**Step-by-step**:
1. List all equipment that must run during outage
2. Record running wattage for each item (nameplate rating)
3. Identify items with motors/pumps/compressors; add starting surge
4. Total continuous running load
5. Add 20-30% safety margin
6. Match to standard generator sizes (round up)

**Professional help**: For larger or critical installations, a professional power audit is recommended. Providers can also consider future expansion needs.

### Can I install my own generator?

**Legally and safely**: No. Generator installation involves:
- Electrical connections (must be done by certified electricians)
- Changeover/ATS wiring (safety critical)
- Fuel systems (fire safety)
- Ventilation and exhaust (health risk if wrong)

**Consequences of DIY**: Insurance issues, safety risk, warranty invalidation, and potential non-compliance with local regulations. Always use certified professionals.

### How long can a generator run continuously?

**Diesel generators** can run 24/7, though:
- Fuel capacity limits continuous runtime without refueling
- Scheduled maintenance (oil/filter changes) required per running hours
- Regular rest periods recommended for inspection

**Design considerations**: For continuous operation, ensure:
- Adequate fuel supply (day tank + supply)
- Sufficient ventilation/cooling
- Routine service schedule based on hours

### Why does my generator need an ATS?

**Manual changeover** requires someone to physically start the generator and switch the load. During an outage:
- Response time depends on availability of personnel
- Human error possible
- Sensitive equipment may suffer

**ATS automation**:
- Restores power in seconds automatically
- Operates 24/7 without staffing
- Reduces equipment stress
- Essential for critical/unattended facilities

**Cost-benefit**: For most commercial and larger residential installations, the ATS cost is fully justified by the reliability and convenience gained.

### What maintenance does a generator need after the rainy season?

**Rainy season post-checks**:
- Inspect for water entry into the generator room or canopy
- Check fuel tank for water (rain can contribute to condensation)
- Battery and electrical connections for corrosion (humidity accelerates)
- Air filter condition
- General cleanliness—moisture and dust combine to cause electrical issues

**In coastal areas** (high humidity/salt air): More frequent checks, corrosion protection, and possibly dehumidification measures recommended.

### How much does it cost to repair a generator?

**Varies widely** by problem:
- Simple service (filters, oil): KES 15,000 - 40,000
- AVR replacement: KES 15,000 - 60,000
- Injector service: KES 10,000 - 30,000 per injector
- Battery replacement: KES 10,000 - 35,000
- Starter/alternator: KES 20,000 - 80,000
- Engine overhaul: KES 80,000 - 500,000+

**Key**: Always obtain a diagnosis and quote before authorizing major repairs. Legitimate providers offer transparent breakdowns.
