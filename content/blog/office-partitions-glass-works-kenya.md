# Office Partitions & Glass Works in Kenya: Complete Guide 2026

## Introduction

How an office is divided determines how well it functions. Partitions shape the flow of work, the sense of space, the penetration of light, and the acoustic environment of every workplace. In Kenya's dynamic commercial sector, offices are increasingly designed for flexibility—and modern partitioning systems make that possible.

From frameless glass partitions that flood interiors with natural light to acoustic drywall dividers that create focused meeting rooms, this guide explores the world of office partitions and glass works in Kenya. It covers system types, material options, costs, installation considerations, and how to choose the right solution for your space.

## Why Partitions Matter

### The Role of Partitions in Modern Offices

**Space Division**: Create rooms, zones, and boundaries within a larger floorplate.

**Privacy**: Meeting rooms, offices, and quiet zones require acoustic and visual separation.

**Light and Openness**: Glass partitions divide space without blocking light, preserving an open, airy feel.

**Flexibility**: Demountable systems allow reconfiguration as teams grow and change.

**Aesthetics**: Partition design contributes significantly to a modern, professional image.

**Function**: Support HVAC, power, and data routing within a neat framework.

### Partitioning vs. Solid Construction

| Consideration | Partition Systems | Solid Construction |
|---------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Speed | Fast (days/weeks) | Slower (weeks/months) |
| Flexibility | Reconfigurable | Fixed |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Light | Glass options keep spaces bright | Blocks light |
| Acoustics | Good (with acoustic systems) | Excellent |
| Cable management | Integrated options | Requires planning |
| Aesthetic | Modern, clean | Traditional |

## Types of Partition Systems

### Frameless Glass Partitions

**The premium modern choice**: Full-height or partial-height glass panels joined with minimal fittings, creating a sleek, unobstructed look.

**Features**:
- **Appearance**: Clean, seamless; maximizes light transmission
- **Configurations**: Fixed panels, sliding doors, hinged doors
- **Glass types**: Clear, frosted/patterned, tinted, laminated (safety), double-glazed
- **Hardware**: Floor-to-ceiling channels, glass-to-glass clamps, minimal frames

**Benefits**:
- Maximum natural light penetration
- Modern, high-end appearance
- Supports open-plan yet separate zones
- Frosted options provide privacy while maintaining light

**Considerations**:
- Higher cost per m²
- Glass cleaning/maintenance
- Acoustics require double-glazed or thicker glass for sound insulation
- Safety glass (laminated or tempered) required by code

**Cost**: KES 20,000 - 45,000 per m² installed depending on glass type, height, and hardware.

### Aluminum Partitioning

**The workhorse system**: Aluminum frames with glass or panel infills—durable, versatile, and cost-effective.

**Features**:
- **Frames**: Powder-coated aluminum (many colors); profiles vary by system
- **Infills**: Clear/tinted/frosted glass, solid panels (MDF/acrylic), combination
- **Doors**: Hinged, sliding, double-action
- **Height**: Full-height or partial (cubicle height)

**Benefits**:
- Versatile and widely available
- Good value
- Durable and low maintenance
- Options for glass or solid panels in one system
- Cable management channels available

**Considerations**:
- Frame visibility (not as seamless as frameless glass)
- Acoustic performance varies with glass thickness and infill
- System quality varies significantly by manufacturer

**Cost**: KES 8,000 - 20,000 per m² installed.

### Acoustic Drywall Partitions

**The standard for privacy**: Metal or timber stud partitions finished with gypsum board, often with acoustic insulation.

**Features**:
- **Construction**: Stud frame (metal/steel most common), insulation (rockwool/mineral fibre), double-layer gypsum board both sides
- **Finishes**: Painted, paper-faced, or with decorative panels
- **Customization**: Can house doors, windows, glazing panels, joinery
- **Height**: Full-height (to ceiling/slab)

**Benefits**:
- Excellent acoustic performance (up to 50+ dB with proper construction)
- Fully customizable (color, openings, glass sections)
- Solid feel; supports shelving/fixtures
- Fire-resistant options

**Considerations**:
- Not easily demountable (semi-permanent)
- Requires finishing (paint/wallpaper)
- Slower than demountable systems

**Cost**: KES 8,000 - 18,000 per m² (including frame, insulation, gypsum, finishing).

### Office Cubicles

**Modular workstations**: Open-plan workspaces divided into individual stations with panels, usually desk-height.

**Features**:
- **Panel heights**: Low (screen) to high (enclosure)
- **Components**: Desks, overhead storage, cable management, task lighting
- **Configurations**: Linear, L-shape, U-shape, clusters

**Benefits**:
- Efficient use of floor space
- Reconfigurable
- Integrated cable management and power/data
- Cost-effective for large teams

**Considerations**:
- Limited privacy/acoustics
- Panel costs add up
- Quality varies widely

**Cost**: KES 25,000 - 80,000 per workstation depending on configuration and quality.

### Sliding and Folding Partitions

**Space flexibility**: Moveable partitions that divide or open a space on demand.

**Features**:
- **Sliding panels**: Track-mounted panels; manual or motorized
- **Folding/wall systems**: Accordion-type folding walls; acoustic versions available
- **Applications**: Training rooms, meeting spaces, event venues, multi-purpose rooms

**Benefits**:
- Maximum space flexibility
- Rapid transformation of room function
- Options for acoustic-rated folding walls

**Considerations**:
- Higher cost (especially acoustic/motorized)
- Track maintenance
- Floor and ceiling tracks required

**Cost**: KES 15,000 - 40,000 per m² depending on system and acoustics.

### Glass Balustrades and Staircases

**Safety and style**: Glass panels for balconies, mezzanines, staircases, and edge protection.

**Features**:
- Frameless glass with clamp or post fixings
- Tempered/laminated safety glass
- Handrail options (glass, timber, metal)

**Applications**: Office mezzanines, stairwells, atriums, residential balconies

**Cost**: KES 25,000 - 50,000 per linear meter installed.

## Glass Selection Guide

### Glass Types for Partitions

**Clear Float Glass**: Standard transparency; for areas where light and visibility are priorities.

**Frosted / Acid-Etched Glass**: Privacy with light; sandblasted or acid-etched finish.

**Patterned Glass**: Decorative textures for visual interest.

**Tinted Glass**: Solar control (bronze, grey, green); reduces glare and heat gain.

**Laminated Glass**: Two+ glass layers bonded with interlayer; safety (doesn't shatter into dangerous shards) and better acoustics.

**Toughened (Tempered) Glass**: Heat-treated for strength; shatters into small safe pieces; required for safety-critical areas.

**Double-Glazed Units (DGU)**: Two panes with air gap; excellent insulation and acoustics; heavier and more expensive.

### Safety Requirements

**Kenya building regulations** require safety glazing where:
- Glass is at floor level or in areas with high foot traffic
- Glass could be mistaken for an opening (fully glazed doors/walls need markings or frames)
- Drop zones (balustrades, staircases)

**Use**: Laminated or toughened glass in these locations.

**Marking**: Glass doors and panels should have visible marking/film at eye level to prevent collisions.

### Glass Thickness Selection

| Application | Typical Thickness |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Interior partition (up to 2.4m) | 8-10mm |
| Full-height partition | 10-12mm |
| Balustrade / staircase | 12-15mm (laminated/toughened) |
| Door panels | 10-12mm (toughened) |
| Double-glazed units | 6+12+6mm etc. |

## Acoustic Performance of Partitions

### Understanding Sound Insulation

**Rw (Weighted Sound Reduction Index)**: Measures how well a partition blocks sound. Higher is better.

| Partition Type | Approx. Rw (dB) |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Single 10mm glass | 30-33 |
| Double-glazed glass (DGU) | 35-40 |
| Single-layer gypsum drywall | 32-36 |
| Double-layer drywall + insulation | 42-48 |
| Double-layer drywall, staggered stud + insulation | 50+ |

### Achieving Good Acoustics

**For glass partitions**:
- Use double-glazed units for meeting rooms
- Seal all edges and joints thoroughly
- Acoustic glass (laminated) improves performance
- Include acoustic-rated glass doors

**For drywall partitions**:
- Full-height (to slab) prevents sound bypass over the ceiling
- Acoustic insulation (mineral wool) in the cavity
- Double layers of gypsum both sides
- Seal all penetrations (cables, sockets, conduits)
- Acoustic seal at floor and ceiling junctions

**Critical**: Sound travels through gaps. Flanking paths (above ceiling, through ducts, under floors) undermine even the best partition.

**Timeline** (typical):
- **Drywall/stud partitions**: 10-25 m² per day per crew

1. Floor and ceiling channels installed and leveled
2. Panels placed and secured
3. Clamps/fittings tightened
4. Door hardware hung and adjusted

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much do glass office partitions cost in Kenya?

**Frameless glass partitions**: KES 20,000 - 45,000 per m² installed, depending on glass type (clear, laminated, double-glazed) and hardware.

**Aluminum-framed glass partitions**: KES 8,000 - 22,000 per m² installed.

**Example**: A 20 m² meeting room with 10mm frameless glass and one glass door: approximately KES 450,000 - 750,000.

### Can I install partitions myself?

**Not recommended**:
- **Glass handling** is hazardous—professional handling and installation are essential for safety
- **Levelling and alignment** require skill and tools
- **Acoustic sealing** must be correct to perform
- **Warranty** usually requires professional installation

**Always use professional installers** for glass partitions. Simple drywall divisions might be DIY-able but are still best left to professionals for quality.

### What is the best partition for a small office?

**Depends on priorities**:
- **Budget**: Aluminum-framed systems
- **Light and modern feel**: Frameless glass (small spaces benefit hugely from light)
- **Privacy/acoustics**: Acoustic drywall
- **Flexibility**: Demountable glass/panel systems

**Pro-Tech tip**: In small spaces, maximize glass to avoid a boxed-in feel; use frosted glass where privacy is needed without sacrificing light.

### How do I soundproof a meeting room?

**Key steps**:
1. Use acoustic-rated partitions (double-glazed glass OR double-layer insulated drywall)
2. Build full-height to the slab (avoid sound passing over the ceiling)
3. Use acoustic-rated doors (solid core with perimeter seals)
4. Seal every gap—joints, sockets, cable penetrations
5. Add absorption inside the room (acoustic panels, carpet, soft furnishings)

**Expect**: Proper construction achieves good speech privacy; no partition is perfectly soundproof without careful detailing.

### What's the difference between tempered and laminated glass?

**Tempered (toughened) glass**:
- Heat-treated for strength (4-5x float glass)
- Breaks into small, dull, relatively safe fragments
- Cannot be cut after tempering (cut to size first)
- Common for safety glass applications

**Laminated glass**:
- Two or more panes bonded with a plastic interlayer
- Breaks but stays in place (interlayer holds fragments)
- Better acoustic and security performance
- Can be cut before lamination

**Which for partitions?** Both are safety-rated. Laminated is preferred for better acoustics and security; tempered for cost-effective strength. High-spec projects often use both (tempered laminated).

### Are partitions fire-rated?

**Standard partitions are generally not fire-rated.** Fire-rated partitions (drywall with fire-resistant board, specific construction) are available for compliance in certain buildings.

**Considerations**:
- Building code and insurance may require fire-rated partitions in specific locations (escape routes, separating uses)
- Fire-rated glazing (wired, ceramic, or special interlayer) differs from standard glass
- Confirm fire requirements with the building's fire consultant/authority

**Tip**: Ask your partition contractor about fire-rated options if your project requires them.

### How long does partition installation take?

**Indicative**:
- **Aluminum partitions**: 10-20 m² per day
- **Frameless glass**: 8-15 m² per day
- **Drywall**: 10-25 m² per day
- **Doors**: 1-2 days per batch

**Example**: A 30 m² glass partition project: roughly 2-4 working days plus door installation.

### Can partitions be reused when I move offices?

**Demountable systems** (many aluminum and some glass systems) are designed for reconfiguration and relocation. Considerations:
- Original installation quality affects ease of reuse
- Hardware and seals may need replacement
- Transport and reinstallation costs

**Fixed systems** (drywall) are not reusable.

**Planning**: If relocation is likely, specify a demountable system and keep installation drawings.

### Do I need planning permission for office partitions?

**Generally, interior partitions that don't alter the building structure or means of escape do not require planning permission.** However:
- Check your lease (some require landlord approval)
- Major changes affecting fire safety or structure may require approval
- Commercial buildings may have specific requirements

**Best practice**: Confirm with your landlord/property manager before installation.

## Designing Your Partition Layout

**Start with how people work**:
- Map team sizes, meeting needs, and collaboration patterns
- Identify which spaces need acoustic privacy (meeting rooms, quiet zones) vs. visual openness (workstations, reception)
- Design circulation first—partitions should frame movement, not block it

**Place glass where it adds value**:
- Perimeter glass preserves natural light deep into the floorplan
- Glass for meeting rooms keeps the office feeling open
- Frosted sections provide privacy at head height
- Glass is a poor acoustic solution—use double-glazing or drywall where sound matters

**Use drywall where privacy matters**:
- Focused work and sensitive conversations need acoustic partitions
- Full-height to slab prevents sound bypass
- Combine solid panels with glazing inserts for light and privacy

**Consider future flexibility**:
- If teams grow/shrink, choose demountable systems
- Design standard module sizes for easy reconfiguration
- Keep electrical/data access points adaptable

**Coordination checklist**:
- Align partition lines with lighting layouts
- Verify power/data outlets planned in new rooms
- Confirm fire alarm coverage (detectors in new enclosed rooms)
- Check HVAC coverage for enclosed spaces
- Verify door swing clearances and corridor widths
