# Technical Repairs & Appliance Servicing in Kenya: Complete Guide 2026

## Introduction

Every business depends on equipment—refrigerators cooling food in restaurants, freezers storing medicines in pharmacies, machines producing goods in factories, and generators keeping operations running. When equipment fails, the cost extends far beyond the repair bill: lost production, spoiled stock, delayed orders, and unhappy customers.

Professional technical repair and appliance servicing keeps these essential machines running reliably. This comprehensive guide explores technical repairs and appliance servicing in Kenya—covering heavy appliances, commercial refrigeration, industrial machinery, HVAC systems, and the preventive maintenance strategies that prevent failures before they happen.

## The Value of Professional Repair Services

### Why Repairs Matter More Than Ever

**Cost of Downtime**:
- A failed commercial fridge can spoil thousands of shillings in stock
- An industrial machine failure can halt an entire production line
- A generator failure during an outage stops the whole business
- Emergency repairs cost more than planned maintenance

**Equipment Lifespan**: Regular servicing extends the life of expensive equipment significantly—often doubling the useful years.

**Safety**: Faulty equipment poses fire, electrical, and mechanical risks. Professional repair ensures safety compliance.

**Warranty Protection**: Many warranties require professional servicing to remain valid.

**Sustainability**: Repairing rather than replacing reduces waste and saves money—a growing priority for businesses and households.

### Repair vs. Replace

| Factor | Repair | Replace |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Cost | Lower (typically) | Higher |
| Age of equipment | Under 7-10 years (typically) | Older, obsolete |
| Extent of failure | Component-level | Major/systemic |
| Efficiency | Existing (may be low) | New (higher) |
| Warranty | Short-term on repair | New warranty |
| Availability of parts | Available | Hard to source |
| Downtime | Shorter | Longer (procurement) |

**Rule of thumb**: If repair cost exceeds ~50% of replacement cost (and the equipment is old), replacement often makes sense. For newer equipment, repair is almost always worthwhile.

## Preventive Maintenance Contracts

### What is a Preventive Maintenance (PM) Contract?

A scheduled servicing agreement where a professional maintains your equipment on a defined schedule—before failures occur.

### What PM Contracts Include

- Scheduled inspections (monthly, quarterly, annual)
- Cleaning and adjustments
- Replacement of consumables (filters, belts, seals)
- Lubrication
- Performance testing
- Documentation and reports
- Priority emergency response (often included)
- Discounted repair rates

### Benefits

- **Reduced downtime**: Failures prevented, not just fixed
- **Extended equipment life**: Regular care keeps equipment efficient
- **Predictable costs**: Fixed contract price vs. surprise breakdown bills
- **Documentation**: Maintenance records support warranties and audits
- **Priority service**: Contract clients get faster emergency response
- **Expert advice**: Ongoing relationship with a technician who knows your equipment

### Who Should Have PM Contracts

- **Businesses with critical equipment**: Hospitals, restaurants, hotels, pharmacies, factories, data centers
- **Multiple units**: Facilities with many appliances/systems
- **High-value equipment**: Industrial machinery, generators, cold rooms
- **Regulated environments**: Food safety, medical, compliance requirements

### PM Contract Pricing (indicative)

- Per visit fee: KES 3,000 - 15,000 (equipment-dependent)
- Annual contracts: KES 30,000 - 150,000+ per major system
- Custom pricing based on equipment inventory and visit frequency

## The Repair Process

### What to Expect When You Call

1. **Diagnosis**: Technician inspects and identifies the fault
2. **Quotation**: Clear quote (parts + labor) before work begins
3. **Approval**: You approve (or decline) the work
4. **Repair**: Professional repair with correct parts
5. **Testing**: Verification that the equipment works correctly
6. **Documentation**: Invoice, warranty on repair, service report

### Service Call Charges

**Typical structure**:
- **Call-out/diagnostic fee**: KES 1,500 - 5,000 (often waived if you proceed with the repair)
- **Labor**: KES 800 - 2,500 per hour (or fixed per-job rates)
- **Parts**: Actual cost + markup
- **Emergency rates**: Higher for out-of-hours/weekend

**Always ask**:
- Is the call-out fee waived if I proceed?
- What is the hourly/labor rate?
- Is there a parts warranty?
- Is the labor warranty included?

### Red Flags to Watch For

- Technician refuses to provide written diagnosis/quote
- Vague pricing ("we'll see when we open it up")
- Pressure to replace rather than repair (without justification)
- No parts warranty offered
- Unregistered/uninsured technicians
- Cash-only demands
- Refusing to provide company details

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does appliance repair cost in Kenya?

**Indicative (2026)**:
- Call-out/diagnostic: KES 1,500 - 5,000
- Refrigerator repair (typical): KES 3,000 - 15,000 (parts vary)
- Washing machine repair: KES 3,000 - 12,000
- Air conditioner servicing: KES 2,000 - 8,000 per unit
- Commercial cold room repair: KES 10,000 - 60,000+
- Generator service: KES 15,000 - 50,000
- Industrial equipment: quoted per job

**Key**: Get a written quote after diagnosis. Costs vary by fault, parts, and equipment.

### How do I know if my appliance is worth repairing?

**Consider**:
- **Age**: Over 10 years old? Replacement may be more cost-effective
- **Repair cost**: If more than ~50% of replacement, lean replace
- **Frequency**: Multiple recent repairs suggest replacing
- **Efficiency**: Old appliances consume more energy
- **Availability of parts**: If parts are hard to source, repair costs rise

**For commercial/industrial equipment**: Consider downtime cost too. Sometimes faster replacement beats long repair timelines.

### Do you repair all appliance brands?

**Professional providers typically service all major brands** and have access to a wide range of spare parts. Confirm before booking that your brand/model is supported.

**For very old or rare equipment**, parts sourcing may take longer—discuss timeline expectations upfront.

### What should I do in an emergency equipment failure?

1. **Safety first**: Switch off the equipment (and power where safe)
2. **Protect contents**: Move perishables to alternative cold storage if possible
3. **Document the fault**: Note symptoms, error codes, sounds
4. **Call a professional**: 24/7 emergency services where available
5. **Don't attempt DIY**: Unqualified repairs risk safety and invalidate warranties
6. **If commercial**: Notify relevant parties (health compliance where applicable)

**Prevention**: An emergency is the worst time to find a technician. Establish a relationship with a reliable provider before you need them.

### How often should equipment be serviced?

**General guidance**:
- **Refrigerators (domestic)**: Annual (clean condenser, check seals)
- **Commercial cold rooms**: Quarterly - semi-annual (critical: monthly temperature log checks)
- **Air conditioners**: 2x per year (before each hot season)
- **Generators**: Per engine hours (typically every 250-500 hours or 6 months)
- **Washing machines**: Annual
- **Industrial machinery**: Per manufacturer schedule (often monthly - quarterly)
- **Water heaters**: Annual (scale/descale, element check)

**Higher-use/commercial equipment**: More frequent servicing.

### Is preventive maintenance really worth the cost?

**Yes—especially for critical equipment**:
- Emergency repairs cost 3-5x more than planned maintenance (rush parts, overtime, lost revenue)
- Well-maintained equipment lasts 30-50% longer
- Regular service catches small problems before they become major failures
- Compliance (food safety, medical) often requires documented maintenance

**Simple example**: A KES 8,000 semi-annual AC service prevents a KES 30,000-80,000 compressor failure—and the business downtime it causes.

### What causes most appliance failures in Kenya?

**Common causes in the Kenyan environment**:
- **Dust and heat**: Condenser coils clog, causing overheating and compressor failure
- **Power fluctuations**: Voltage instability damages compressors, electronics, motors
- **Power surges**: After outages, surges damage unprotected equipment
- **Improper use**: Overloading, blocked airflow, poor installation
- **Age and wear**: Natural component degradation
- **Refrigerant leaks**: Especially from vibration/damage or poor installation
- **Poor-quality parts**: Aftermarket parts failing early

**Mitigation**: Surge protection, clean environment, regular servicing, quality installation.

### Can I fix appliances myself?

**For simple tasks** (filter cleaning, seal replacement, unblocking drains) you can follow manufacturer guidance. For anything involving:
- Compressors and refrigerant (specialized equipment, regulated)
- Electrical components (safety hazard)
- Gas appliances (dangerous)
- Complex electronics

**Always call a professional.** DIY repair on complex equipment risks: injury, further damage, warranty loss, and safety hazards (fire, gas, electrical).

### How do I choose the right repair company?

1. **Referrals**: Ask trusted contacts
2. **Reviews**: Check online reputation

5. **Warranties**: Confirm both parts and labor warranties

**Pro-Tech tip**: For commercial/critical equipment, choose a provider offering maintenance contracts and emergency response—not just one-off repairs.

## Spare Parts: Genuine vs. Aftermarket

**Why parts quality matters**:
- Compressors, control boards, and motors are precision components—quality determines reliability
- A "cheap" part that fails in months costs more than a genuine part lasting years (labor + downtime)

**Genuine (OEM) parts**:
- Made by or for the original manufacturer
- Guaranteed fit and performance
- Higher cost; longer warranty (where offered)
- Best for critical components (compressors, electronics, safety parts)

**Aftermarket parts**:
- Made by third-party manufacturers
- Wide quality range—from good branded alternatives to poor imitations
- Cheaper; availability often better
- Acceptable for some consumables (filters, belts) if from reputable brands

**How to choose**:
- **Critical/safety components**: Use genuine parts
- **Consumables**: Quality branded aftermarket is usually fine
- **Electronics/control boards**: Prefer genuine to avoid compatibility issues
- **Ask your technician**: Which parts are you fitting, and why?

**Red flags**: A technician who insists on unnamed "compatible" parts for everything, refuses part receipts, or quotes unusually low part prices.

**Right to know**: Always ask to see the parts being fitted and request the old parts back. Legitimate providers keep full transparency.

## Preparing for a Technician Visit

**Before the visit**:
- Note the equipment brand, model, and serial number
- Record symptoms (when it failed, what it does, error codes if shown)
- Clear access to the equipment
- Gather any existing maintenance records

**During the visit**:
- Ask what the diagnosis is and the repair plan
- Request a written quote before work begins
- Confirm the warranty on parts and labor
- Ask about preventing recurrence

**After the visit**:
- Test the equipment yourself
- Keep the invoice and service report
- Note the warranty terms and service provider contact
- Update your maintenance records

**For businesses**: Authorize one responsible person to manage repair visits, approve quotes, and sign off work—this prevents disputes and keeps records consistent.

## Repair Warranties and Guarantees

**What to expect**:
- **Parts warranty**: Typically 3-12 months on genuine parts (varies by part and provider)
- **Labor warranty**: Usually 30-90 days on the work performed
- **Combined terms**: Some providers offer 3-6 month warranties covering both parts and labor

**Key questions to ask**:
- How long is the warranty on parts and on labor?
- What does the warranty cover (and exclude)?
- Does the warranty require service through the same provider?
- Who bears the cost if a "repaired" fault recurs?

**Keep documentation**: Always retain invoices, service reports, and warranty certificates. They protect you if faults recur and support any claims.

**Pro-Tech tip**: A provider confident in its work welcomes warranty questions. Vague or unwritten warranty promises are a warning sign—get terms in writing.
